About

Neurons, Math and
the gap between them.

I'm a Computer Science & Engineering student (IoT specialisation) entering my third year. My work is in computational neuroscience --specifically neural dynamics, stochastic modeling, and information-theoretic analysis of spike train data.

I started by replicating published neuron models from scratch, writing the differential equations directly rather than using simulators, because I wanted to understand what the math was actually doing.

I'm interested in how biological neural systems process and transmit information, and how that breaks down in disease. Most of what I know I've built outside formal coursework.

Stochastic Neuron Models — Biological Benchmarking

Jan – Feb 2026

Stochastic FitzHugh-Nagumo and LIF models benchmarked against Allen Institute cortical spike train data. Euler-Maruyama and Heun integration, ensemble statistics (100 trials/condition), CV/Fano factor/KS-distance validation.

Key Findings

  • Stochastic FHN models exhibit moderate variability but underestimate biological CV.
  • Standard LIF produces highly regular firing, highlighting limitations for cortical spike variability.
  • Noise type and model dimensionality significantly influence spike statistics.
